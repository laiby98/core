Scope and rationale

Our approved subject is Home Assistant's Energy integration, located in homeassistant/components/energy. We focus on the path by which household electricity statistics are configured, validated, stored, and exposed to the Energy dashboard. The main scenario is consumption from the electrical grid; solar production, battery flow, device-level consumption, and calculated cost are included where they clarify that scenario.

The manifest identifies Energy as a calculated system integration and declares websocket_api, history, and recorder as dependencies. This establishes an important boundary: Energy does not communicate directly with a physical meter. Other integrations produce sensor states and long-term statistics; Energy selects and validates those statistics, stores user preferences, calculates supporting values such as costs, and supplies results to the frontend.

The scope provides a meaningful end-to-end path without requiring the group to recover all of Home Assistant. It also exposes clear boundaries between the frontend, Core, persistence/statistics services, and device integrations.

Complementary techniques

1

Systematic repository search and code reading

We begin at manifest.json, follow async_setup() to the registered WebSocket handlers, and trace ws_save_prefs() into EnergyManager.async_update(), delayed storage, update listeners, generated sensors, and validation. Targeted symbol searches and nearby tests reveal static responsibilities, module boundaries, and candidate steps for the group's diagrams and trace.

2

Focused execution and test-based experimentation

We run narrow existing tests for behavior found during reading. The selected tests exercise integration setup, WebSocket requests, default and updated preferences, delayed storage, generated cost-sensor information, and the configured-state check. Reading explains how the path is intended to work; executable assertions independently confirm that the selected path is exercised.
